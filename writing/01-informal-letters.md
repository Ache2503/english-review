# Informal Letters and Emails - Writing Practice

## Structure of Informal Letters/Emails

### 1. Greeting
- Hi [Name],
- Hello [Name],
- Dear [Name],

### 2. Opening
Ask how they are:
- How are you?
- How are things?
- I hope you're well.
- How's everything going?

### 3. Main Body
Write about your topic in paragraphs:
- **Paragraph 1**: Respond to their news or introduce your topic
- **Paragraph 2**: Give details and examples
- **Paragraph 3**: Add more information or ask questions

### 4. Closing
- I'd better go now.
- I must finish now.
- Write back soon!
- Looking forward to hearing from you.
- Take care!

### 5. Sign-off
- Love,
- Best wishes,
- See you soon,
- Cheers,
- All the best,

[Your name]

## Useful Phrases

### Thanking
- Thank you/Thanks for your letter/email.
- It was great to hear from you.
- Thanks for inviting me...

### Giving news
- Guess what!
- You'll never believe what happened...
- I've got some great/bad news.

### Apologizing
- I'm really sorry that...
- I'm afraid I can't...
- Sorry I haven't written for so long.

### Making suggestions
- Why don't we...?
- How about...?
- We could...
- Would you like to...?

### Asking for help/advice
- I need your help with...
- Can you give me some advice about...?
- What do you think I should do?

## Writing Prompts

### Prompt 1: Invitation
Write an email to your friend inviting them to your birthday party. Include:
- The date, time, and place
- What you plan to do
- Ask them to confirm if they can come

### Prompt 2: Holiday
Write a letter to your English-speaking friend about your last holiday. Include:
- Where you went and who you went with
- What you did there
- What you liked most about it

### Prompt 3: Thank you
Write an email thanking your friend for letting you stay at their house. Include:
- Thank them for their hospitality
- Mention something specific you enjoyed
- Suggest meeting again soon

### Prompt 4: Request for advice
Write to a friend asking for advice about learning a new skill (like playing guitar, cooking, or a sport). Include:
- Why you want to learn it
- What problems you're having
- Ask for their suggestions

### Prompt 5: Sharing news
Write to a friend telling them about a new job/school/house. Include:
- Your news and how you feel about it
- Details about the new situation
- Invite them to visit or meet

## Example: Invitation Email

**Subject: My Birthday Party!**

Hi Maria,

How are you? I hope everything is going well with your new job!

I'm writing to invite you to my birthday party. I'm having a party at my house on Saturday, March 15th, at 7:00 PM. We're going to have dinner, listen to music, and play some games. A few of our friends from university will be there too.

I really hope you can come! It would be great to see you and catch up. Can you let me know by next week if you can make it? If you need my address, just text me.

Looking forward to seeing you!

Best wishes,
Carlos

---

**Word count**: 113 words
**Tone**: Friendly and informal
**Structure**: Clear greeting, invitation details, closing

## Practice Exercise

Choose one of the prompts above and write your own informal letter or email. Remember to:
- Use an appropriate greeting and closing
- Include all the required information
- Write in a friendly, natural style
- Use 100-150 words
- Organize your ideas in paragraphs

## Self-Check Questions

After writing, ask yourself:
- ✓ Did I include all the required information?
- ✓ Is my tone appropriately informal?
- ✓ Did I use paragraphs correctly?
- ✓ Did I check for grammar and spelling errors?
- ✓ Would a friend enjoy receiving this letter?
