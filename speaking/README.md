# Speaking Practice - B1 Level

Develop your speaking skills with these topics and activities.

## Speaking Strategies

### Preparation
- **Think before you speak**: Organize your thoughts
- **Use simple language**: Say what you can say, not what you can't
- **Don't translate**: Think directly in English
- **Practice regularly**: Speak a little every day

### During Speaking
- **Speak clearly**: Pronunciation is more important than accent
- **Use body language**: Gestures help communication
- **Don't rush**: It's okay to speak slowly
- **Self-correct**: If you make a mistake, fix it
- **Ask for clarification**: "What does that mean?" "Can you repeat that?"

### After Speaking
- **Reflect**: What went well? What was difficult?
- **Note mistakes**: Learn from errors
- **Celebrate progress**: Notice improvements
- **Set goals**: What do you want to work on next?

## Speaking Topics

### Personal Information
1. Talk about yourself, your family, and your hometown
2. Describe your daily routine
3. Talk about your hobbies and interests
4. Describe your home
5. Talk about your job or studies

### Experiences
1. Describe a memorable holiday
2. Talk about a special event in your life
3. Describe an interesting person you've met
4. Talk about a problem you solved
5. Describe learning something new

### Preferences and Opinions
1. What kind of music/films/books do you like? Why?
2. Talk about your favorite restaurant or food
3. Discuss advantages and disadvantages of social media
4. Express your opinion about learning languages
5. Talk about your ideal job or lifestyle

### Future Plans
1. What are your plans for the weekend/next year?
2. Where would you like to travel? Why?
3. What would you like to learn or achieve?
4. How do you see your future?
5. If you could live anywhere, where would you choose?

### Hypothetical Situations
1. What would you do if you won the lottery?
2. If you could meet anyone, who would it be and why?
3. What would you do if you could travel back in time?
4. If you could have any superpower, what would it be?
5. What would you change about your city?

## Speaking Activities

### Activity 1: Self-Recording
Record yourself speaking about a topic for 2-3 minutes. Listen back and note:
- Vocabulary: Did you use varied words?
- Grammar: Were there any errors?
- Fluency: Did you speak smoothly or with many pauses?
- Pronunciation: Were words clear?

### Activity 2: Picture Description
Look at a photo and describe:
- What you can see
- Where it might be
- What's happening
- How people might be feeling
- What might happen next

### Activity 3: Role Play
Practice common situations:
- At a restaurant (ordering food)
- At a hotel (checking in)
- At a shop (buying something)
- At a doctor (describing symptoms)
- Making a phone call (arranging to meet)

### Activity 4: Topic Discussion
Choose a topic and speak for 2 minutes. Structure:
- **Introduction**: State your main idea
- **Body**: Give 2-3 supporting points with examples
- **Conclusion**: Summarize your opinion

### Activity 5: Story Telling
Tell a story about:
- Something funny that happened
- A memorable journey
- An interesting coincidence
- Meeting someone new
- Learning from a mistake

## Useful Speaking Phrases

### Expressing Opinions
- I think/believe that...
- In my opinion...
- It seems to me that...
- I feel that...
- Personally, I would say...

### Agreeing
- I agree completely.
- That's true.
- Exactly!
- I think so too.
- You're absolutely right.

### Disagreeing (Politely)
- I'm not sure I agree with that.
- I see what you mean, but...
- That's an interesting point, however...
- I understand, but I think...
- Actually, I have a different view.

### Adding Information
- Also...
- Moreover...
- In addition...
- Another point is...
- Besides that...

### Giving Examples
- For example...
- For instance...
- Such as...
- Like...

### Giving Reasons
- Because...
- Since...
- Due to...
- The reason is...

### Hesitation Devices (Natural Pauses)
- Well...
- You know...
- Let me think...
- How can I put it...
- What I mean is...

### Correcting Yourself
- I mean...
- What I wanted to say is...
- Actually...
- Sorry, let me rephrase that...

## Pronunciation Tips

### Focus Areas
1. **Word stress**: understand vs UNderstand
2. **Sentence stress**: Important words are stressed
3. **Intonation**: Rising (questions) vs falling (statements)
4. **Linking**: Words connect in natural speech
5. **Difficult sounds**: th, r, v, w

### Practice Techniques
- **Mirror practice**: Watch your mouth movements
- **Record and compare**: Record yourself and compare with native speakers
- **Tongue twisters**: "She sells seashells by the seashore"
- **Minimal pairs**: ship/sheep, bit/beat, cat/cut
- **Shadowing**: Repeat immediately after audio

- □ I can describe experiences and events
- □ I can express opinions and give reasons
- □ I can have a simple conversation on familiar topics
- □ I can make and respond to suggestions
- □ I can tell a simple story
- □ I can explain something I've learned
- □ I can ask for and give clarification

## Progress Goals

Set specific, measurable goals:
- **Example 1**: "I will practice speaking for 10 minutes every day this week."
- **Example 2**: "I will record myself discussing three different topics this month."
- **Example 3**: "I will have a 5-minute conversation with a language partner twice a week."
- **Example 4**: "I will learn and use 10 new phrases for expressing opinions."

Remember: Everyone makes mistakes when learning to speak. Mistakes are part of the learning process. The most important thing is to practice regularly and not be afraid to speak!
