# Common Phrasal Verbs - B1 Level

## What are Phrasal Verbs?

Phrasal verbs are verbs combined with particles (prepositions or adverbs) that create a new meaning.

**Example**: 
- **look** = to use your eyes to see something
- **look after** = to take care of someone/something

## Types of Phrasal Verbs

### Separable
The object can go between the verb and particle OR after the particle.
- Turn **off** the TV = Turn the TV **off** ✓
- Turn **it off** ✓ (Turn **off it** ❌ with pronouns)

### Inseparable
The object must go after the particle.
- Look **after** the children ✓ (Look the children **after** ❌)

## Common Phrasal Verbs by Category

### Daily Life

**get up** - to wake and get out of bed
- I **get up** at 7 AM every day.

**wake up** - to stop sleeping
- I **woke up** late this morning.

**put on** - to wear, to place clothing on your body (separable)
- **Put on** your jacket, it's cold.
- **Put** your shoes **on**.

**take off** - to remove clothing (separable)
- **Take off** your shoes before entering.
- **Take** them **off** at the door.

**turn on** - to start a device (separable)
- Can you **turn on** the light?
- **Turn** it **on**, please.

**turn off** - to stop a device (separable)
- Don't forget to **turn off** the TV.
- **Turn** it **off** when you leave.

**tidy up** - to make neat and organized (separable)
- We need to **tidy up** the house.
- Let's **tidy** it **up** before guests arrive.

**clean up** - to make clean (separable)
- Please **clean up** your room.

### Relationships

**get on (with)** - to have a good relationship
- I **get on well with** my colleagues.
- Do you **get on** with your neighbors?

**fall out (with)** - to have an argument and stop being friends
- They **fell out** over money.

**look after** - to take care of (inseparable)
- Can you **look after** my dog this weekend?

**grow up** - to become an adult
- I **grew up** in a small town.
- Where did you **grow up**?

**bring up** - to raise/educate children (separable)
- She **brought up** three children alone.

**split up** - to end a relationship
- They **split up** after two years together.

**make up** - to become friends again after an argument
- They had a fight but **made up** the next day.

### Work and Study

**carry on** - to continue
- **Carry on** with your work.

**give up** - to stop trying, to quit (separable)
- Don't **give up**! Keep trying.
- He **gave up** smoking last year.

**work out** - to find a solution, to exercise (separable)
- We need to **work out** a plan.
- I **work out** at the gym three times a week.

**figure out** - to understand or solve (separable)
- I can't **figure out** how this works.
- Can you **figure** it **out**?

**write down** - to note on paper (separable)
- **Write down** your phone number.
- Let me **write** it **down**.

**look up** - to search for information (separable)
- **Look up** the word in a dictionary.
- **Look** it **up** online.

**hand in** - to submit (separable)
- **Hand in** your homework by Friday.

**fill in/out** - to complete a form (separable)
- Please **fill in** this form.
- **Fill** it **out** and return it.

### Communication

**call back** - to return a phone call (separable)
- Can you **call** me **back** later?

**hang up** - to end a phone call
- She got angry and **hung up**.

**speak up** - to speak louder
- Could you **speak up**? I can't hear you.

**shut up** - to stop talking (informal/rude)
- He told them to **shut up**.

**bring up** - to mention a topic (separable)
- Don't **bring up** that subject.

**get through** - to successfully make contact by phone
- I couldn't **get through** to you yesterday.

### Movement and Travel

**get in/into** - to enter (a car, taxi)
- **Get in** the car.

**get on** - to board (bus, train, plane)
- We **got on** the wrong bus.

**get off** - to leave (bus, train, plane)
- **Get off** at the next stop.

**set off/out** - to begin a journey
- We **set off** early in the morning.

**pick up** - to collect someone (separable)
- I'll **pick you up** at 8 PM.

**drop off** - to leave someone at a place (separable)
- Can you **drop me off** at the station?

**take off** - (of a plane) to leave the ground
- The plane **took off** on time.

**check in** - to register at a hotel/airport
- We **checked in** at 2 PM.

**check out** - to leave a hotel
- We have to **check out** by 11 AM.

### Problems and Solutions

**break down** - to stop working (machine), to cry
- My car **broke down** on the highway.
- She **broke down** in tears.

**sort out** - to resolve a problem (separable)
- We need to **sort out** this issue.

**come up** - to arise (a problem)
- Something has **come up**, I can't meet you.

**deal with** - to handle (inseparable)
- I'll **deal with** it tomorrow.

**run out of** - to have no more of something
- We've **run out of** milk.

**cut down on** - to reduce
- I'm trying to **cut down on** sugar.

## Tips for Learning Phrasal Verbs

1. **Learn in context**: Study phrasal verbs in example sentences
2. **Group by topic**: Learn related phrasal verbs together
3. **Note separability**: Remember which are separable/inseparable
4. **Use them**: Practice using phrasal verbs in speaking and writing
5. **Visual aids**: Create images or situations to remember meanings
6. **Review regularly**: Phrasal verbs need repeated practice

## Common Mistakes

❌ Turn off it. → ✓ Turn it off. (pronoun must go in the middle)
❌ Look after the children them. → ✓ Look after them.
❌ I get on good with him. → ✓ I get on well with him. (use adverb 'well')
❌ I woke up in 7 AM. → ✓ I woke up at 7 AM. (use 'at' with specific times)
