# Modal Verbs

## What are Modal Verbs?

Modal verbs are special verbs that modify the meaning of the main verb. They express ability, possibility, permission, obligation, advice, etc.

**Common Modals**: can, could, may, might, must, should, will, would, shall

## Characteristics of Modal Verbs

1. They don't change form (no -s, -ed, -ing)
2. They are followed by the base form of the verb (infinitive without "to")
3. They don't need auxiliary verbs (do/does/did)

✓ She **can swim**. (not "can to swim" or "cans swim")
✓ He **should go**. (not "should to go")

## Uses of Modal Verbs

### CAN

**Ability** (present)
- I **can speak** three languages.
- She **can't drive** a car.

**Permission** (informal)
- **Can** I use your phone?
- You **can** leave early today.

**Possibility**
- It **can** be very hot in summer here.

### COULD

**Ability** (past)
- I **could swim** when I was five.
- She **couldn't understand** the instructions.

**Polite request**
- **Could** you help me, please?
- **Could** I have some water?

**Possibility** (less certain than can)
- It **could rain** tomorrow.
- This **could** be a problem.

### MAY

**Permission** (formal)
- **May** I come in?
- You **may** use the library.

**Possibility** (50% certain)
- It **may** be true.
- She **may not** come to the party.

### MIGHT

**Possibility** (less than 50% certain)
- I **might go** to the cinema tonight. (I'm not sure)
- They **might not** arrive on time.

### MUST

**Obligation** (strong)
- You **must** wear a seatbelt.
- Students **must** hand in homework on Monday.

**Logical deduction** (certainty)
- She **must be** tired after working all day.
- They **must know** each other well.

**Negative**: "must not/mustn't" = prohibition
- You **mustn't** smoke here. (It's forbidden)

### HAVE TO

**Obligation** (external rule)
- I **have to** go to work. (It's my job)
- Do you **have to** wear a uniform?

**Past**: had to
**Negative**: don't have to = not necessary
- You **don't have to** come if you don't want to. (It's not necessary)

### SHOULD / OUGHT TO

**Advice** (recommendation)
- You **should** see a doctor.
- You **shouldn't** eat so much sugar.
- You **ought to** exercise more.

**Expectation**
- She **should** be here by now. (I expect her to be here)
- The train **should** arrive at 5 PM.

### WILL / WON'T

**Future**
- I **will** call you tomorrow.
- The shop **won't** open until 10 AM.

**Offers/Promises**
- I**'ll** help you with your homework.
- I promise I **won't** be late.

**Predictions**
- It **will** rain tomorrow.

### WOULD

**Polite requests**
- **Would** you close the window, please?

**Hypothetical situations** (Second conditional)
- If I had money, I **would** travel.

**Past habits**
- When I was young, I **would** play in the park every day.

## Degrees of Certainty

**100% Certain**
- must, can't (negative certainty)

**Very Likely**
- should, ought to, will

**Possible**
- may, could, might

**Example**: Looking at dark clouds
- It **will** rain. (I'm very sure)
- It **should/ought to** rain. (I expect it)
- It **may/might/could** rain. (It's possible)

## Practice Exercises

### Exercise 1: Choose the correct modal

1. I __________ (can/must) speak English and Spanish.
2. You __________ (must/should) stop at a red light. (It's the law)
3. __________ (May/Would) I borrow your pen?
4. She __________ (might/must) be at home, but I'm not sure.
5. You __________ (don't have to/mustn't) wear a tie, but you can if you want.
6. He __________ (could/must) play the piano when he was six.
7. You __________ (should/must) see that movie. It's great!
8. I __________ (will/should) help you tomorrow, I promise.
9. __________ (Could/Must) you speak more quietly, please?
10. It's 10 PM. The shops __________ (must/might) be closed now.

### Exercise 2: Must or Have to?

1. I __________ go to the dentist tomorrow. (I have an appointment)
2. You __________ be quiet in the library. (It's a rule)
3. __________ you __________ work on Saturdays?
4. Students __________ wear a uniform at this school.
5. I __________ finish this report by Friday. (My boss said so)

### Exercise 3: Should or Must?

1. You __________ try this restaurant. The food is delicious! (recommendation)
2. You __________ have a visa to enter this country. (requirement)
3. You __________ eat more vegetables. (advice)
4. Passengers __________ fasten their seatbelts. (obligation)
5. You __________ apologize to her. (advice)

### Exercise 4: Correct the mistakes

1. He can speaks English very well. ❌
2. She must to go home now. ❌
3. You no must smoke here. ❌
4. Do I should call him? ❌
5. They couldn't to find the address. ❌
6. You don't must worry about it. ❌

## Summary Table

| Modal | Main Use | Example |
|-------|----------|---------|
| can | ability/permission | I can swim. |
| could | past ability/polite request | I could run fast. Could you help? |
| may | permission/possibility | May I leave? It may rain. |
| might | possibility (less certain) | It might snow. |
| must | strong obligation/certainty | You must stop. She must be tired. |
| have to | obligation | I have to work. |
| should | advice/expectation | You should rest. |
| will | future/promise | I will come. |
| would | polite request/hypothetical | Would you help? I would go. |
