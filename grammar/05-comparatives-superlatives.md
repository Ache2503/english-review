# Comparatives and Superlatives

## What are Comparatives and Superlatives?

**Comparatives** are used to compare **two** things, people, or groups.
**Superlatives** are used to show that something has the **most** of a quality in a group of three or more.

### Examples:
- **Comparative**: My car is **faster than** your car. (comparing 2 cars)
- **Superlative**: This is the **fastest** car in the race. (comparing 3+ cars)

## How to Form Comparatives and Superlatives

### One-Syllable Adjectives

**Comparative**: adjective + **-er** + **than**
**Superlative**: **the** + adjective + **-est**

| Adjective | Comparative | Superlative |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| tall | taller than | the tallest |
| old | older than | the oldest |
| fast | faster than | the fastest |
| cheap | cheaper than | the cheapest |
| young | younger than | the youngest |

**Examples**:
- John is **tall**, but Mike is **taller than** John. Peter is **the tallest** of all.
- This book is **cheap**, that one is **cheaper than** this one, but the red one is **the cheapest**.

### Two-Syllable Adjectives Ending in -y

**Comparative**: change **-y** to **-ier** + **than**
**Superlative**: **the** + change **-y** to **-iest**

| Adjective | Comparative | Superlative |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| happy | happier than | the happiest |
| easy | easier than | the easiest |
| funny | funnier than | the funniest |
| busy | busier than | the busiest |
| pretty | prettier than | the prettiest |

**Examples**:
- She is **happier than** she was last year.
- This is **the easiest** exercise in the book.
- That was **the funniest** movie I've ever seen.

### Two or More Syllable Adjectives

**Comparative**: **more** + adjective + **than**
**Superlative**: **the most** + adjective

| Adjective | Comparative | Superlative |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| expensive | more expensive than | the most expensive |
| interesting | more interesting than | the most interesting |
| difficult | more difficult than | the most difficult |
| beautiful | more beautiful than | the most beautiful |
| comfortable | more comfortable than | the most comfortable |

**Examples**:
- This car is **more expensive than** that one.
- Math is **the most difficult** subject for me.
- She is **the most beautiful** woman I know.

### Irregular Comparatives and Superlatives

Some adjectives have irregular forms that you must memorize:

| Adjective | Comparative | Superlative |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| good | better than | the best |
| bad | worse than | the worst |
| far | farther/further than | the farthest/furthest |
| little | less than | the least |
| much/many | more than | the most |

**Examples**:
- This restaurant is **good**, but that one is **better**, and the Italian one is **the best**.
- My result was **bad**, yours was **worse**, but his was **the worst** of all.
- I have **more** books than you, but she has **the most** books in the class.

## Spelling Rules

### Double the final consonant
When a one-syllable adjective ends in consonant + vowel + consonant, double the final consonant:

- big → bi**gg**er → the bi**gg**est
- hot → ho**tt**er → the ho**tt**est
- thin → thi**nn**er → the thi**nn**est
- fat → fa**tt**er → the fa**tt**est

**Exception**: Don't double w, x, or y (new → newer, gray → grayer)

### Drop the -e
When an adjective ends in -e, just add -r or -st:

- large → larger → the largest
- nice → nicer → the nicest
- wide → wider → the widest

## Using Comparatives and Superlatives

### Comparatives

**Structure**: Subject + verb + comparative + than + object

- My house is **bigger than** yours.
- She runs **faster than** I do.
- This exercise is **more difficult than** the last one.

**Without "than"**: When the comparison is clear from context
- I need a **bigger** bag.
- Do you have a **cheaper** option?

### Superlatives

**Structure**: Subject + verb + **the** + superlative + (in/of + group)

- She is **the tallest** student **in** the class.
- This is **the most expensive** hotel **in** the city.
- He is **the best** player **on** the team.

**Note**: Use **the** before superlatives (exceptions: when used with possessives like "my best friend")

## Intensifying Comparatives

You can make comparatives stronger:

- **much/a lot/far** + comparative
  - This is **much better** than that.
  - She's **a lot taller** than her sister.
  - It's **far more expensive** than I thought.

- **a little/a bit/slightly** + comparative
  - This one is **a little bigger**.
  - She's **slightly older** than me.

## Comparing Equal Things

To say two things are the same, use: **as + adjective + as**

- John is **as tall as** Mike. (They're the same height)
- This car is **as expensive as** that one.
- She's **as intelligent as** her brother.

**Negative**: **not as + adjective + as** OR **not so + adjective + as**
- This book is **not as interesting as** that one.
- He's **not so fast as** he used to be.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ **Don't use "more" with -er ending**
- ~~more easier~~ → easier
- ~~more better~~ → better

❌ **Don't use "most" with -est ending**
- ~~most cheapest~~ → cheapest
- ~~most fastest~~ → fastest

❌ **Don't forget "the" with superlatives**
- ~~She is tallest~~ → She is the tallest

❌ **Don't use "than" with "as...as"**
- ~~as tall than~~ → as tall as

❌ **Don't double consonants after two vowels**
- ~~cheapper~~ → cheaper (ea = two vowels)
